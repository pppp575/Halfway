      Why halfway is harder than it looks

      On a flat map, a straight line between two cities looks like the shortest way to travel. It isn’t. The Earth is round, so the shortest route is a curve called a great circle, and on most world maps it bows toward the nearest pole. That’s why long-haul flights look curved on airport screens.

      It also means the true middle is often nowhere near where your eye expects it. Halfway between London and Tokyo isn’t over Iran or India. It’s in northern Siberia, close to the city of Norilsk.

      Each day brings five new pairs of cities, the same for everyone. The first round is a short hop, and the last one usually crosses an ocean. Every round is worth up to 100 points, based on how far your pin lands from the real halfway point compared with the length of the route.

      How to play

      You get two cities. Tap the map where you think the exact halfway point between them is, then lock in your pin.

      Halfway means halfway along the shortest route over the globe, the path a plane would fly. On a flat map those routes curve, so the middle is often not where it looks. After each round the map folds into a globe so you can see why.

      Each round is worth up to 100 points. There are five rounds a day and everyone plays the same cities.
